# Route Lab rules scope — 2026 NFL season

Authority: [2026 Official Playing Rules of the NFL](https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/2026-nfl-rulebook/). Route Lab is a deterministic teaching simulation. It is not certified NFL officiating software or calibrated to tracking data.

| Area | Implemented in Full Game / Free Play | Remaining limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Field, series, possession | 100-yard field plus both 10-yard end zones, directional yard lines, first downs, losses, fourth-down changes, interceptions and returns, touchdowns and safeties. Fumbles and backward passes stay live as loose balls, with spatial pursuit and recovery, forward/backward out-of-bounds spots, end-zone touchbacks/safeties, interception momentum spots, and restricted teammate advance on fourth down or after the two-minute warning. Exhibition begins at an animated coin toss; a setup jump can choose quarter, clock, score, possession and field position. Next advances the game with situation-based CPU calls, while the player can override either play. | One loose-ball release is modeled per scrimmage snap; intentional batting, complicated impetus changes, and every turnover exception remain outside this slice. |
| Game timing | Four 15-minute quarters, halftime, 10-minute regular-season overtime on a tie, three timeouts per half, two in overtime, two-minute warnings, and a simulated 27–35 second line reset after running-clock snaps (capped below the play clock). | Clock operation is snap-by-snap, not a real-time stadium clock. The full Rule 4 timing and 10-second runoff exceptions are not present. Overtime uses simplified toss and possession resolution. |
| Scoring | Six-point touchdowns, 1-point kick tries, 2-point scrimmage tries, 3-point field goals, 2-point safeties, kickoff after scores, safety kick. | Tries do not cover every defensive return or penalty enforcement case. |
| Special teams | Seeded kickoff and punt distance, 22-player full-field replay with moving rushers, protectors, blockers, coverage players and returner, live muff and blocked-kick recovery, fair-catch call, return touchdowns, declared onside kicks, field goals and extra points. Dynamic kickoff arrays use the 2026 restraining lines, nine receivers in the setup zone, five on the receiving restraining line and two deep; coverage stays in place until the ball lands. Early kicking-team movement causes a five-yard rekick. Short free kicks give the receiving team the favorable landing or 25-yard award (30 for safety kicks); touchbacks use the 35/20-yard distinctions. | Player ratings and contact are stylized. Fair-catch interference, every kick-catch and first-touching case, live penalty options, fair-catch kicks, and all blocked-try and missed-kick exceptions remain incomplete. |
| Officials | Seven officials shown on the tactical field; referee calls on scoring, spots, downs, timeouts, period changes, formation errors, encroachment, snap anticipation, and kick outcomes. Spatial defender contact can produce DPI or illegal contact; sustained blocker engagement against a beating rusher can produce offensive holding. The play report identifies the player, time, and location. A single foul has accept/decline choices, with an automatic next-play choice; opposing live-ball fouls before a possession change offset and replay the down. Near the line to gain, the chain crew measures the marked spot. Coaches can challenge a marked spot before the next snap, with replay-official control in the final two minutes, overtime and a fourth-down short ruling. Replay compares the underlying unrounded frame spot and changes a first-down/possession ruling only on clear evidence; challenge counts and timeout loss apply. Game book retains calls. | Contact classification is stylized; no full illegal-block taxonomy, all penalty enforcement spots, fouls after a possession change, safety fouls, substitutions, or exhaustive replay review of catch, goal-line, boundary, clock and penalty administration. Chain measurement is a simplified half-yard on-field mark with precise replay spot. |
| Scrimmage players | The existing 11-on-11 engine moves blockers, rushers, receivers, coverage defenders, runners, QBs, and returners in individual snaps. Awareness, pursuit, tackling, route and throwing ratings affect behavior; special moves appear in the report. Each fresh exhibition randomizes both teams' ratings; the home depth chart selects the actual players dressed for snaps. Generated jersey numbers stay within position ranges, with unique numbers per team. | The 2D model does not decide every real-world catch, contact, blocking, eligibility, or personnel rule. Reduced-player campaign drills remain training scenarios. |

## Implementation slices

1. **Exhibition setup and game rhythm — implemented:** start menu (Free, Campaign, Load), editable saved call/color, animated opening toss, randomized teams and home depth chart, CPU starters if skipped, situation jump, one-button next-play loop, snap-to-snap clock runoff, snap-anticipation offside and a geometry-based defensive pass interference call. This is a single exhibition without season W/L tracking.
2. **Live-ball possession — implemented:** rated contact and handoff fumbles, a designed backward-pass control and Pitch Back play, player pursuit and recovery of a live loose ball, forward/backward out-of-bounds enforcement, own/opponent end-zone safety or touchback rulings, teammate-advance limits on fourth down and after two minutes, and interception touchback/momentum spots. The replay and report show the loose-ball sequence.
3. **Special teams — implemented:** fair-catch call and dead-ball spot, catch muff and blocked-kick recovery, spatial 22-player return replay and touchdowns, dynamic kickoff and onside alignment, early-movement rekick, landing-zone enforcement and 2026 touchback spots. The game report gives the kick events, contacts, recovery and ruling.
4. **Officiating — implemented as a scoped pass:** spatial DPI and illegal contact plus contact-based offensive holding; player/time/spot evidence in replay and report; accept/decline choices with a one-button default; pre-change-of-possession double fouls; close chain measurements; coach or booth spot review with challenge and timeout limits. Full Rule 14 and Rule 15 administration remains future work.
5. **Timing and overtime:** full Rule 4 stop/start/runoff exceptions, substitution administration, two-minute and overtime edge cases.

The app must not be described as implementing **all** current NFL rules. Further rule work should begin with the official rule text and a deterministic game-state scenario, then add observable on-field behavior where relevant.
