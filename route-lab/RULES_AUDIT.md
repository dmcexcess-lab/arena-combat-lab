# Route Lab rules scope — 2026 NFL season

Authority: [2026 Official Playing Rules of the NFL](https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/2026-nfl-rulebook/). Route Lab is a deterministic teaching simulation. It is not certified NFL officiating software or calibrated to tracking data.

| Area | Implemented in Full Game / Free Play | Remaining limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Field, series, possession | 100-yard field plus both 10-yard end zones, directional yard lines, first downs, losses, fourth-down changes, interceptions and returns, touchdowns and safeties. Exhibition begins at an animated coin toss; a setup jump can choose quarter, clock, score, possession and field position. Next advances the game with situation-based CPU calls, while the player can override either play. | Exact forward progress, fumbles, muffed kicks, lateral passes, and all touchback/turnover edge cases are not modeled. |
| Game timing | Four 15-minute quarters, halftime, 10-minute regular-season overtime on a tie, three timeouts per half, two in overtime, two-minute warnings, and a simulated 27–35 second line reset after running-clock snaps (capped below the play clock). | Clock operation is snap-by-snap, not a real-time stadium clock. The full Rule 4 timing and 10-second runoff exceptions are not present. Overtime uses simplified toss and possession resolution. |
| Scoring | Six-point touchdowns, 1-point kick tries, 2-point scrimmage tries, 3-point field goals, 2-point safeties, kickoff after scores, safety kick. | Tries do not cover every defensive return or penalty enforcement case. |
| Special teams | Seeded kickoff and punt distance, kick/return spot, 35-yard kickoff and 20-yard punt touchbacks, returner lane decisions against 11 coverage players and 10 blockers, adjustable kicker/punter/returner/unit ratings, declared onside kicks, field goals and extra points. Kick and return paths appear on the full-field map and execution factors appear in the report. | Kick returns are resolved as a small spatial model, without the full 22-player replay. Fair catches, blocked kicks, return touchdowns, detailed 2026 kickoff alignment/landing-zone penalties, and every missed-field-goal or safety-kick exception are absent. |
| Officials | Seven officials shown on the tactical field; referee calls on scoring, spots, downs, timeouts, period changes, formation errors, alignment-based encroachment, seeded snap anticipation by a close-aligned rusher, spatially detected defensive pass interference, and kick outcomes. Game book retains calls. | Other live-ball penalties, challenges/replay, chain measurement, substitutions, player-safety fouls, and all enforcement options are absent. |
| Scrimmage players | The existing 11-on-11 engine moves blockers, rushers, receivers, coverage defenders, runners, QBs, and returners in individual snaps. Awareness, pursuit, tackling, route and throwing ratings affect behavior; special moves appear in the report. Each fresh exhibition randomizes both teams' ratings; the home depth chart selects the actual players dressed for snaps. Generated jersey numbers stay within position ranges, with unique numbers per team. | The 2D model does not decide every real-world catch, contact, blocking, eligibility, or personnel rule. Reduced-player campaign drills remain training scenarios. |

## Implementation slices

1. **Exhibition setup and game rhythm — implemented:** start menu (Free, Campaign, Load), editable saved call/color, animated opening toss, randomized teams and home depth chart, CPU starters if skipped, situation jump, one-button next-play loop, snap-to-snap clock runoff, snap-anticipation offside and a geometry-based defensive pass interference call. This is a single exhibition without season W/L tracking.
2. **Live-ball possession:** fumbles, recovery scrambles, backward passes, loose balls in end zones, exact touchback and turnover spots.
3. **Special teams:** fair catch, muff and block recovery, full return replay and touchdowns, dynamic kickoff alignment and landing-zone enforcement.
4. **Officiating:** more live-ball fouls with who/where/when evidence, enforcement choices, offsetting fouls, challenges, replay and measured spots.
5. **Timing and overtime:** full Rule 4 stop/start/runoff exceptions, substitution administration, two-minute and overtime edge cases.

The app must not be described as implementing **all** current NFL rules. Further rule work should begin with the official rule text and a deterministic game-state scenario, then add observable on-field behavior where relevant.
