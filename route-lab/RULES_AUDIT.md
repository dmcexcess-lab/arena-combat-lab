# Route Lab rules scope — 2026 NFL season

Authority: [2026 Official Playing Rules of the NFL](https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/2026-nfl-rulebook/). Route Lab is a deterministic teaching simulation. It is not certified NFL officiating software or calibrated to tracking data.

| Area | Implemented in Full Game / Free Play | Remaining limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Field, series, possession | 100-yard field plus both 10-yard end zones, directional yard lines, first downs, losses, fourth-down changes, interceptions and returns, touchdowns and safeties. Fumbles and backward passes stay live as loose balls, with spatial pursuit and recovery, forward/backward out-of-bounds spots, end-zone touchbacks/safeties, interception momentum spots, and restricted teammate advance on fourth down or after the two-minute warning. Exhibition begins at an animated coin toss; a setup jump can choose quarter, clock, score, possession and field position. Continue selects the situation-based CPU calls; Run play executes the snap or staged fourth-down kick. Editing the call before Run play overrides the CPU decision. | One loose-ball release is modeled per scrimmage snap; intentional batting, complicated impetus changes, and every turnover exception remain outside this slice. |
| Game timing | Four 15-minute quarters, halftime, 10-minute regular-season overtime on a tie, three timeouts per half, two in overtime, and two-minute warnings. Continuing from a play now simulates a 27–35 second line reset, capped below a 25-second administrative play clock when applicable, deducts that time when the game clock runs, and records it in the game book. A subsequent scrimmage snap or punt/field-goal kick does not deduct the same reset twice. A stopped game clock remains stopped through the reset. | Clock operation is event-based, not a real-time stadium clock. Rule 4 exceptions remain incomplete for substitutions, fouls, replay administration, untimed downs and some runoff choices. Overtime does not cover all modified sudden-death foul and possession exceptions. |
| Scoring | Six-point touchdowns, 1-point kick tries, 2-point scrimmage tries, 3-point field goals, 2-point safeties, kickoff after scores, safety kick. | Tries do not cover every defensive return or penalty enforcement case. |
| Special teams | Seeded kickoff and punt distance, 22-player full-field replay with moving rushers, protectors, blockers, coverage players and returner, live muff and blocked-kick recovery, fair-catch call, return touchdowns, declared onside kicks, field goals and extra points. Dynamic kickoff arrays use the 2026 restraining lines, nine receivers in the setup zone, five on the receiving restraining line and two deep; coverage stays in place until the ball lands. Early kicking-team movement causes a five-yard rekick. When a fair-catch signal is active, the kick replay checks the returner's catch window for actual coverage-player contact; interference awards a fair catch and advances the receiving team 15 yards from the contact spot. Seeded lateral kick drift can produce an untouched free-kick sideline crossing; the receiver gets the more favorable of the inbounds sideline spot and 25 yards from the kick spot. A short missed field goal can fall inbounds beyond the line and be caught by the deep returner for a live return; an untouched miss retains the kick-spot/receiver-20 dead-ball award. Short free kicks give the receiving team the favorable landing or 25-yard award (30 for safety kicks); touchbacks use the 35/20-yard distinctions. | Player ratings and contact are stylized. Touching before a kick goes out, end-zone/out-of-bounds edge cases, every kick-catch and first-touching case, fair-catch kicks, and muffed or touched missed kicks, blocked tries, and other missed-kick exceptions remain incomplete. |
| Officials | Seven officials shown on the tactical field; referee calls on scoring, spots, downs, timeouts, period changes, formation errors, encroachment, snap anticipation, and kick outcomes. Spatial defender contact can produce DPI, illegal contact or defensive holding; a delayed rusher can rough the passer; actual tackle and support-block contacts can produce a facemask or block-in-the-back foul. Sustained blocker engagement against a beating rusher can produce offensive holding. A lineman facing a close rusher can move before the snap for a false start. The play report identifies the player, time, and location. A single foul has accept/decline choices, with an automatic next-play choice; opposing live-ball fouls before a possession change offset and replay the down. Near the line to gain, the chain crew measures the marked spot. Coaches can challenge a marked spot before the next snap, with replay-official control in the final two minutes, overtime and a fourth-down short ruling. Replay compares the underlying unrounded frame spot and changes a first-down/possession ruling only on clear evidence; challenge counts and timeout loss apply. Game book retains calls. | Contact classification is stylized; no full illegal-block taxonomy, all penalty enforcement spots, fouls after a possession change, safety fouls, or exhaustive replay review of catch, goal-line, boundary, clock and penalty administration. Chain measurement is a simplified half-yard on-field mark with precise replay spot. |
| Scrimmage players | The existing 11-on-11 engine moves blockers, rushers, receivers, coverage defenders, runners, QBs, and returners in individual snaps. Awareness, pursuit, tackling, route and throwing ratings affect behavior; special moves appear in the report. Each fresh exhibition randomizes both teams' ratings; the home depth chart selects the actual players dressed for snaps. Three candidates per slot allow automatic replacement after a generated medical event. Contact and movement exposure from replay frames produce medical evaluations, limited availability or game removal; the replay and report identify the action and substitution. Generated jersey numbers stay within position ranges, with unique numbers per team. | The 2D model does not decide every real-world catch, contact, blocking, eligibility, or personnel rule. Reduced-player campaign drills remain training scenarios. |

## Implementation slices

1. **Exhibition setup and game rhythm — implemented:** start menu (Free, Campaign, Load), editable saved call/color, animated opening toss, randomized teams and home depth chart, CPU starters if skipped, situation jump, opening coin-toss action that executes the kickoff, one-button next-play loop, snap-to-snap clock runoff, snap-anticipation offside and a geometry-based defensive pass interference call. This is a single exhibition without season W/L tracking.
2. **Live-ball possession — implemented:** rated contact and handoff fumbles, a designed backward-pass control and Pitch Back play, player pursuit and recovery of a live loose ball, forward/backward out-of-bounds enforcement, own/opponent end-zone safety or touchback rulings, teammate-advance limits on fourth down and after two minutes, and interception touchback/momentum spots. The replay and report show the loose-ball sequence.
3. **Special teams — implemented:** fair-catch call and dead-ball spot, catch muff and blocked-kick recovery, spatial 22-player return replay and touchdowns, dynamic kickoff and onside alignment, early-movement rekick, landing-zone enforcement and 2026 touchback spots. The game report gives the kick events, contacts, recovery and ruling.
4. **Officiating — implemented as a scoped pass:** spatial DPI and illegal contact plus contact-based offensive holding; player/time/spot evidence in replay and report; accept/decline choices with a one-button default; pre-change-of-possession double fouls; close chain measurements; coach or booth spot review with challenge and timeout limits. Full Rule 14 and Rule 15 administration remains future work.
5. **Timing, health and overtime — partial implementation:** contact- and exertion-based injury events with automatic depth-chart replacement; injury timeouts and late offensive runoff; close-rusher false starts, defensive holding, roughing the passer, facemask and block in the back; 25-second administration after possession changes; out-of-bounds restart windows; overtime toss, both initial possession opportunities, sudden death after the conditions in Rule 16, and overtime two-minute warning. Full Rule 4 and Rule 16 administration remains open.
6. **Special-teams officiating — implemented as a bounded case:** a valid fair-catch call activates a frame-based returner contact check during the kick flight. A coverage player who enters the catch window and makes contact after the signal can be flagged; the receiver is awarded the fair catch and 15 yards from the observed contact spot. This does not cover every kick-related foul or fair-catch-kick procedure.
7. **Free-kick out-of-bounds ruling — implemented as a bounded case:** kick direction drifts laterally using seeded error influenced by the kicker's accuracy. If an untouched kickoff, safety kick or declared onside kick crosses a sideline before landing, the receiver automatically elects the more favorable of the sideline inbounds spot and 25 yards from the kick spot; the replay and report record the crossing and award. This does not model player touching before the ball goes out or all end-zone/out-of-bounds interactions.
8. **Fielded short missed field goal — implemented as a bounded case:** a seeded short miss can remain inbounds beyond the line of scrimmage, where receiving players pursue the flight and the deep returner may catch it; a successful catch uses the existing live return, tackle and touchdown logic. An untouched miss still uses the established kick-spot or receiver-20 spot. The model does not yet handle muffed or touched missed kicks, wide misses, boundary/goalpost rulings, or every missed-kick exception.
9. **Progression and field-position display — implemented:** accepted scrimmage results update game state while remaining pending for any ruling. Continue selects the next CPU calls; Run play performs the next snap or the staged CPU fourth-down kick. Editing the play before the snap replaces the staged CPU action. Replay views follow the same live ball spot and show the next line of scrimmage at the final frame.
10. **Game correctness — partial implementation:** pre-snap fouls use a new attempt seed on each no-snap retry; post-possession-change tackle logic no longer applies the offensive runner tackle loop to a defensive recovery; exhausted depth slots and unavailable specialists block the play; tries are untimed and throwaways stop the clock; QB decisions receive the actual to-gain distance. Full game-path and rules calibration remains open.
11. **Ball exchanges and possession — bounded implementation:** ordinary snaps animate from the center to a reachable QB; the QB retains the ball until the pass arrives; a run handoff occurs only after QB and runner reach the designed mesh. Unreachable snap and mesh landmarks fail validation, and a missed live mesh leaves the QB carrying the ball. Loose-ball rulings wait for recovery, boundary exit, or a settled ball with no player in recovery distance. A 20-second engine watchdog rejects unresolved snaps with a diagnostic and leaves game state unchanged; it does not award a whistle or a gain. Backward pitches remain loose-ball arcs with player recovery rather than guaranteed teammate transfers. Full snap fumbles, controlled catch contests, ball-contact physics and broader possession rules remain open.
12. **Runner and run-fit reads — implemented as a bounded spatial read:** the ball carrier samples projected defender locations, called-track leverage and blockers ahead, then records whether they press, cut or bounce with the lane evidence in the play report. Run-fit defenders keep their assigned gap until the back declares away from it; awareness controls scrape response and pursuit influences the intercept angle. The model remains a short-horizon 2D heuristic without true blocking-scheme keys, read-option choices, or calibrated player-tracking behavior.
13. **Pass-rush technique and stunt — bounded implementation:** rushers select a bull rush, swim or rip from their ratings when they engage a pass blocker; a seeded technique matchup can stall or shed the blocker and is shown in the play report. Cover 0 Blitz runs one inside-penetrator/outside-looper exchange, with its execution logged. Move counters, hand placement, protection calls, other stunt families and calibrated rush win rates remain out of scope.
14. **Receiver tracking and catch point — bounded implementation:** the targeted receiver adjusts toward the projected ball during flight based on awareness and hands. At arrival, catch and disruption estimates use receiver hands/awareness/route skill, defender coverage/hands/awareness/agility, and relative spacing; catches, drops, breakups, interceptions, and boundary misses retain visible outcomes in the play report. Body control, footwork, true high-point contests, and full catch rules remain unmodeled.
15. **Offensive line combo help — bounded implementation:** adjacent eligible blockers can recognize a teammate engaged with the same threatening defender, occupy a second leverage point, and increase combined blocking resistance. Combo contacts appear in the report. This remains a proximity-based 2D response without full slide calls, combination rules, hand placement, or pocket geometry.

The app must not be described as implementing **all** current NFL rules. Further rule work should begin with the official rule text and a deterministic game-state scenario, then add observable on-field behavior where relevant.

## Frequency evidence and limits

The 2025 NFL regular-season statistics report about 6.37 accepted penalties per team per game (about 12.74 combined). [NFL team statistics](https://static.clubs.nfl.com/image/upload/packers/cxglgc64ods58hymylgr). The NFL also reported 538 in-game concussion evaluations and 26 medical timeouts in 2025; those are evaluations/stoppages, not counts of every in-game injury or confirmed concussion. [NFL 2025 health and safety data](https://www.nfl.com/_amp/2025-season-key-takeaways).

Route Lab uses seeded per-contact and per-movement exposure hazards, influenced by player ratings and actual play geometry; it does not schedule a fixed number of injuries or flags and offers no frequency slider. These published totals are reference bounds, not a claim that this prototype matches the NFL distribution. The simulation still omits many foul classes, special-teams medical events, full rosters, medical diagnosis and return-to-play protocols. The eight-game fixed-seed audit on the build before this exchange slice averaged 9.9 generated flags and 2.75 medical events over 197.5 plays per game. The exchange slice audit averaged 9.75 generated flags and 2.625 medical events over 173.625 plays; all eight games completed. These are small samples, not a match to NFL play volume or accepted-penalty definitions. A larger, definition-matched rate audit and game-pace calibration are still required before calling its injury or penalty frequency realistic.
