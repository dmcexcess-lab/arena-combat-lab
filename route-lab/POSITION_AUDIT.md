# Route Lab position and decision audit

Evaluated on the deterministic 11-on-11 engine after the 2026-11-06 depth pass. Evidence includes all 88 offensive/defensive preset matchups at seed 71, a full Shotgun Mesh vs 4–3 Cover 2 snap at seed 700, and isolated counterfactual snaps. A participation record reports distance moved and contact time from replay frames, so a listed assignment alone is not counted as execution.

| Position | What actually happens | Current depth / gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| QB | Drops, senses nearby rush based on awareness, chooses an escape lane, evaluates projected receivers and pass lanes, then throws, throws away, hands off, or takes a sack. Awareness changes pressure recognition and risk weighting; accuracy and power change the throw. | Reads are one simplified decision window. No full progression across multiple seconds, protection calls, cadence, or true option read. |
| RB / ball carrier | Receives a timed handoff, follows the designed run path, samples projected defender positions, the called track, and blocker leverage ahead, then records a press, cut, or bounce read with its lane evidence. After a catch, a receiver uses the same open-field lane search. Agility, vision, strength, tackling matchup, and seed govern juke, spin, or stiff arm attempts. | Gap choice remains a short-horizon local heuristic. No ball security, true cutback plant animation, full run-blocking scheme, or calibrated tracking data. |
| X, Z, slot WR | Execute actual waypoint routes with acceleration and route-running effects; the intended target adjusts toward the projected catch point during flight according to awareness and hands. Catch outcomes consider receiver tracking, hands, defender coverage/ball skills, and both players' arrival spacing; the play report shows the matchup and result. A receiver can advance, evade, and get tackled. Nearby non-carriers can switch to support blocking after a catch. | No true body positioning, footwork, sideline toe taps, high-pointing, or specialized contested-catch moves. Slot now receives WR defaults, not safety defaults; X/Z now use the receiver speed branch. |
| TE | Runs an eligible route or blocks according to the selected job, using TE strength/blocking and a speed between WR and OL. Can become the carrier and attempt a stiff arm. | No separate chip-and-release, inline leverage, or route conversion logic. |
| LT, LG, C, RG, RT | Move to their assigned threat or pass/run gap; when an adjacent blocker is already engaged with a threatening defender, they can add a combo block from a separate leverage position. The help increases the pair's resistance and appears in the report. LG can pull on a run. | Combos use proximity and engagement rather than real protection calls; no pocket integrity, hand placement, full slide protection, or detailed blocking fouls. The center is validated at the snap, but snap exchange is abstracted. |
| DE / DT / NT / EDGE | Rush or pursue according to job; pass-rush rating affects closing speed and strength contests blockers. On pass snaps they choose a bull rush, swim, or rip from their ratings and contest the blocker with a seeded matchup; a win can shed contact and accelerate pressure. Cover 0 Blitz coordinates an inside penetrator with an outside looper and reports the exchange. They can sack before release and pursue a live run/catch. | Techniques and the stunt are bounded 2D contests; no hand placement, counter chains, true gap assignments, or full protection/stunt communication model. |
| LB / MLB / OLB | Zone, man, blitz, spy, or run-fit assignments move the player; run fits hold their assigned gap until the back declares away, then awareness controls how quickly they scrape and pursuit affects the intercept angle. Once a run breaks the line or a catch occurs, they pursue and can tackle. Play Action Cross sells a QB/back mesh; run-fit awareness affects the fake read. | Gap keying uses carrier position and a simple declared-path threshold, not true guard/tackle keys, scrape exchanges, or full option responsibility. Zone exchange remains coarse; Play Action Cross is a short single-fake read. 3v1 training drills can create unrealistic breakaways. |
| CB / nickel | Zone or man assignment moves to threats; coverage/awareness affect read reaction and the arrival at the catch point. At the pass point the nearest defender's coverage, hands, awareness and leverage contest the receiver's tracking, hands and route skill. Match-zone defenders preserve route ownership in overlaps, then transfer a crossing route to the next zone defender. Man assignments can press at the line. After a catch they pursue and tackle. Nickel has DB defaults rather than linebacker defaults. | Press remains a short rating-based release contest without detailed hand-fighting or true sideline technique. Zone exchange remains an area-and-route heuristic, not a complete pattern-match ruleset. Catch geometry is a simplified 2D contest. |
| FS / SS | Hold a designated deep or zone responsibility before the catch; react to a live carrier, keep top leverage when approaching from ahead, and pursue/tackle. | Safety rotation, disguise, deep help decisions, and angle selection remain approximate. |

## Measured checks

- The seed-700 11-on-11 replay produced participation records for all 22 players; all 22 moved more than 0.5 yard and offensive/defensive contact was recorded. This establishes executed spatial behavior for that snap, not comprehensive football competence.
- In an isolated run, putting a defender in the called gap shifted the RB more than a yard laterally by 2.00 seconds; moving that defender outside left the RB close to the called lane. The RB continued beyond a 15-yard final waypoint.
- Against the same completed pass, changing SS awareness and pursuit from 1 to 99 changed his position shortly after the catch and reduced the final gain in the deterministic seed-700 example.
- Against one unblocked rusher, the high-awareness QB recognized pressure earlier and avoided a sack in a fixed seed where the low-awareness QB was sacked.
- Juke, stiff arm, and spin are attempted only near a defender. Success changes runner or defender coordinates and the defender's tackle recovery; failure slows the runner. Each attempt and outcome is recorded in `specialMoves`, the event log, and the play result panel.

## Remaining realism limits

This is a causal spatial teaching simulator, but it is not calibrated NFL game film or a full football engine. The RPO Glance preset currently always follows its designated run, and Play Action Cross uses a short awareness-based fake rather than a complete keyed defensive read; the HB Screen lacks a full release/block timing model. Pass gains remain high in some zone matchups, so yard distributions should not be presented as NFL-like. See [RULES_AUDIT.md](RULES_AUDIT.md) for the rules boundary.
