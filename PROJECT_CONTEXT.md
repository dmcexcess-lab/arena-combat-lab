## Hosted sibling prototype

Core economy/combat/contracts/persistence/offline/balance rules remain unchanged. `test_scene_ui.js` now gates stateful motion, outside queue geometry, staff routing, nine screens and eight diegetic hotspots.

## Hosted Route Lab prototype
`route-lab/index.html` is a self-contained football X/O play editor and deterministic simulation prototype, published to GitHub Pages at `/route-lab/`. The existing Pages workflow copies it alongside Arena and Tavern Keeper; this does not alter Godot runtime behavior. It includes 11 core offensive and 8 defensive presets, plus an optional Pitch Back play, 22-player presentation, editable routes/assignments, formation validation, and replay controls. Added six-lesson Offense and Defense campaigns with small-sided training scrimmages (explicit relaxed training formation legality) progressing to full 11v11. The offense finale runs a touchdown drive with first downs; the defense finale runs a four-down defensive stand. Player changes are checked against lesson baselines and deterministic simulation outcomes, with progress in `route-lab-campaign-v1` localStorage. Source modules `engine.js`, `plays.js`, `campaign.js`, and `app.js` are retained for maintenance, while `index.html` embeds them for standalone Pages deployment; update both when changing the application. CI executes `route-lab/tests/campaign.test.cjs`. Added `encyclopedia.js`: categorized, searchable glossary with cross-links from every campaign clinic, available in all modes. Each of the twelve stages now has a three-section coaching explanation, a three-task workshop, replay question, and knowledge quiz; both live-play objective and quiz are required. QB pass logic evaluates predicted catch locations, defender separation, throwing lanes, read priorities, pressure and situational depth, uses seeded viable-read selection and writes `decisions[]` for the Film Room. The seed input increments each new Run; entering an old seed reproduces a matchup. `route-lab/tests/knowledge.test.cjs` verifies content and read variation. This is an alpha approximation, not yet fully NFL-calibrated.

## Route Lab player and contact model
The same `GridironCore.simulate` drives free play and campaign drills. Its 1–99 player model now includes stamina, pass rush, pursuit, route running and runner vision alongside the original ratings. Sprinting and blocking accumulate within-play fatigue; acceleration, agility and route running affect cuts; runner speed and vision affect path execution; defenders use rated reaction and pursuit to track assignments. Block strength now contests pass rush, a tackler has a recovery period after a missed attempt, and defenders contest the projected catch point. QB passes lead moving receivers through an iterative flight estimate. Replay frames include remaining player energy. These are deterministic educational approximations; fatigue resets at each snap and is not yet a persistent drive condition.

## Route Lab live-ball and 2026 rules pass
After a catch, the carrier chooses an open-field lane and keeps running, nearby offensive players offer support blocks, and all defenders enter pursuit. An interception remains live for a return. Campaign downs count negative yards and defensive possession changes; basic end-zone sacks/tackles can produce safeties. Full-squad backfield players wearing ineligible numbers must report eligible. `route-lab/RULES_AUDIT.md` maps implemented 2026 rulebook slices to the remaining gaps. This remains a scrimmage teaching simulation, not a fully NFL-compliant game engine.

## Route Lab live-ball possession slice
`GridironCore.simulate()` releases a moving loose ball on rated contact strips, sack strips, muffed handoffs, or a designed backward pass. Nearby players on both teams pursue and recover from their actual frame positions; a recovery can continue as a return. `GridironPlays` includes Pitch Back and the editor exposes the target and release time. `RouteGame.finishScrimmage()` rules on the resulting dead-ball spot, including forward/backward fumble out of bounds, end-zone touchbacks and safeties, restricted teammate advance on fourth down and after the two-minute warning, and interception end-zone/momentum spots. The report and game book expose the possession ruling. One loose-ball release is modeled per snap; `route-lab/RULES_AUDIT.md` identifies remaining exceptions.

## Route Lab special teams slice
`RouteGame.kick()` now returns a time-indexed 22-player record for free kicks, punts, field goals and tries. Kicking and receiving teams have actual coverage, rush/protection and returner locations; blockers engage nearby coverage players, the returner reads lanes and can break tackles or score. Muffs and blocked kicks remain live for spatial pursuit and recovery. `app.js` plays these frames on the full field with a slider, event log and ratings report; a receiving-call selector allows a fair catch or explicit return. The 2026 kickoff layout is generated from restraining/setup/landing-zone lines, coverage stays put until landing, and an early-movement flag moves the kick back five yards for a rekick. Short free kicks grant the receiving team the favorable legal spot, with a 30-yard award for safety kicks. This is still a stylized teaching model; see `route-lab/RULES_AUDIT.md` for open kick and foul exceptions.

## Route Lab officiating slice
`RouteGame.finishScrimmage()` now derives a no-foul result, samples actual frame engagement and receiver contact for offensive holding, illegal contact and DPI, and returns accept/decline states for single live-ball fouls. Opposing fouls before a possession change offset and replay the down. The report and replay events show actor, time and spot. Near a line to gain, officials mark the ball in half-yard increments and a chain measurement appears; coach or replay-official review compares the underlying frame spot, with challenge limits and timeout loss for unsuccessful coach reviews. `app.js` keeps the pending ruling for choice/review, persists it across Load, and Next accepts the default. This is a bounded Rule 14/15 teaching model: post-possession enforcement, other foul classes, and catch/boundary/goal-line replay remain open in `route-lab/RULES_AUDIT.md`.

## Route Lab timing, health and frequency slice
`GridironCore.simulate()` now records individual blocking and tackle contacts alongside movement exposure. `RouteGame.finishScrimmage()` uses those actual participants and seeded per-exposure hazards to produce medical events, substitute among three depth-chart candidates, record a game book entry, and apply injury-timeout/runoff rules in late-game cases. Frame contact can also produce defensive holding, roughing the passer, a facemask or block in the back; a line player facing a nearby rusher can false start. Defender break on the airborne ball, pursuit and tackle geometry were tightened, and ordinary first-down CPU calls now mix runs and short passes with deeper routes. Out-of-bounds clock restarts now vary by game period, administrative changes of possession use a 25-second play clock, and regular-season overtime records a seeded toss, both initial possession opportunities and subsequent decisive scores. No user-facing event-frequency control exists. `route-lab/RULES_AUDIT.md` retains the distinction between measured NFL references and uncalibrated simulation rates; full Rule 4/16 and medical rules are not implemented.

## Route Lab between-play reset
Committing a completed play now calls `RouteGame.resetToLine()` before the next play is selected. It immediately deducts a simulated 27–35 second reset (capped under a 25-second administrative play clock) from a running game clock, logs the elapsed time, and uses `lineReady` so the following snap or scrimmage kick does not deduct it again. Stopped-clock resets leave the game clock unchanged. The reset can trigger the two-minute warning or end a period before the next snap; no walk-back animation is required.

## Route Lab fair-catch officiating
On free kicks and punts, a fair-catch call enables an in-flight catch-window check. If a coverage player actually reaches within contact distance of the returner while the kick is catchable after the signal, the seeded spatial replay returns a Fair Catch Interference foul. It awards a dead-ball fair catch 15 yards from the observed contact spot, logs the player/time/evidence, and reports it alongside the kick. Other kick fouls, accept/decline, and fair-catch kicks remain incomplete.

## Route Lab free-kick sideline ruling
Kickoff, safety-kick and declared onside-kick trajectories now use seeded lateral error influenced by kicker accuracy. When an untouched kick crosses a sideline before landing, `RouteGame.kick()` records the crossing and the officials give the receiving team the more favorable legal choice between the inbounds sideline spot and 25 yards from the kick spot. The special-teams report names both the ruling and awarded spot. This bounded case does not model a player touching the kick before it goes out or every end-zone/out-of-bounds interaction; see `route-lab/RULES_AUDIT.md`.

## Route Lab missed field-goal return slice
A seeded short field-goal miss may land inbounds beyond the line of scrimmage. Receiving players pursue the ball during its flight; a receiving player within catch range can secure it and run through the existing return/tackle path, including a possible return touchdown. Uncaught or longer misses retain the untouched missed-field-goal dead-ball spot. The report identifies a fielded miss. This is a bounded Rule 11 case; muffed/touched misses and boundary/goalpost exceptions remain open in `route-lab/RULES_AUDIT.md`.

## Route Lab kickoff entry and field-position display
The opening coin-toss CPU-lineup action now resolves the toss and immediately runs the kickoff; the equivalent depth-chart Done action does likewise, while the situation-jump flow does not start a kick. The lower tactics field overlay names the current ball spot from the game state and places a ball marker at the line of scrimmage, matching the full-field overview. `Next play` continues to execute the current CPU-selected call unless the player overrides it.

## Route Lab play progression display correction
A settled scrimmage result is now the displayed game state while remaining pending for the next lineup and penalty/review choices. The full-field marker tracks the replay ball from the saved snap spot; the tactics grid stays rooted at that snap and labels the matching live ball spot. Next and Set next play are disabled during active replay so a click cannot cancel a snap; after the replay, Next resets to the resulting state, picks CPU plays, and runs the next snap.
